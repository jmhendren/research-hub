Scale-across — the networking and optics layer that lets AI training span data centers — is real and quantifiable, but only with scope discipline: the defensible 2030 segment is $6.3B inside a $4.4B–$9.2B band, while quoted "market sizes" span roughly 20x because they describe different universes. The supply chain is sold out into 2028, and the one risk that breaks the demand thesis itself — rather than reshuffling who captures it — is asynchronous training.

What it means

  • From a ~$2.0B 2025 base, the scoped IPoDWDM / coherent ZR/ZR+ pluggable-optics segment compounds at 25.7% to a $6.3B Base in 2030, inside a $4.4B–$9.2B band; a flat-ASP bottom-up brackets it from above at $8.4B, with ASP deflation the reconciling variable.
  • Quoted market sizes span roughly 20x because they describe different universes — Dell'Oro's $19B all-optical-transport, MarketsandMarkets' $25.89B broad DCI, LightCounting's $100B all-AI-cluster-optics upside are all legitimate numbers, and none is the scale-across enablement segment.
  • Supply, not demand, sets revenue through 2027: scale-across laser components are effectively sold out, Ciena queues nearly all new orders for 2027 delivery with equilibrium not before 2028, and Coherent is quadrupling InP capacity over two years.
  • The one risk that breaks the demand thesis rather than reshuffling it: Google DeepMind's Decoupled DiLoCo trained a 12B-parameter model across four US regions on 2–5 Gbps of ordinary WAN — if low-communication training scales to frontier size, the 10–14x inter-DC bandwidth premise collapses.
