The edge-inference "knock-on" optical wave is real but tiered and largely not-yet-at-the-edge. The measured money is hyperscaler scale-across (campus + long-haul, North-America-concentrated), captured by scale systems vendors (Ciena/Nokia/Cisco) and components (Marvell/Coherent/Fabrinet). Non-scale vendors (Smartoptics, Ekinops, ADTRAN, Ribbon) reliably win only the fragmented regional/metro/municipal tail — with zero wins at the hyperscaler or marquee-neocloud tier. Ribbon's "67% rural DCI" is a relabel of a Pew facility count, not a spend share.

What it means

  • Tiered capture, not a smooth gradient: scale systems (Ciena/Nokia/Cisco) and components (Marvell/Coherent/Fabrinet) take the hyperscaler, neocloud and long-haul money tiers; non-scale vendors win only the fragmented regional/metro/municipal tail — with zero wins at the hyperscaler or marquee-neocloud tier.
  • Real but not-yet-at-the-edge: no analyst house sizes an edge-optical line; the booming DCI number is hyperscaler campus-cluster and long-haul, while the metro/regional layer the edge thesis needs is flat-to-declining.
  • Only one of five candidate mechanisms is operative today as an outward push (latency-sensitive inference), and even it rides existing fiber plus new GPUs rather than new third-party optical orders.
  • Ribbon's "67% of US DCI growth is rural" relabels Pew's facility-count finding; capacity-weighted data refutes the spend implication — Northern Virginia alone holds ~3.5x all secondary US markets combined.
