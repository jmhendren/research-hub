1. Audience & anchor question

This primer is for a smart-finance reader who has heard "Circle is the USDC company" but does not yet have the vocabulary to underwrite the equity. By the end you can: explain how a stablecoin issuer earns revenue; quantify Circle's dependence on rates and on Coinbase; place USDC against Tether; read the GENIUS Act's effect on the moat; and judge whether ~10× revenue is fair.

The anchor question: if stablecoins are becoming real dollar infrastructure, is Circle the way to own that — or a rate-and-distribution sandwich priced like a software company?

2. What Circle is — a regulated float business

Circle issues USDC, a token redeemable 1:1 for dollars; it invests the dollars users hand over and keeps the interest — a money-market-fund-like float business, not a software or payments company.

A stablecoin is a blockchain token designed to hold a constant value — for USDC, exactly one U.S. dollar. A user gives Circle $1, Circle mints 1 USDC, and holds the $1 in reserve. The token pays the holder nothing. What Circle does with the pooled dollars — invest them in short-term Treasuries and earn interest — is the business.

The scale is now meaningful: USDC circulation reached $77.0B at end-Q1'26 (+28% YoY)S001, a new all-time high after collapsing from ~$55B in late 2022 to ~$24B in mid-2023 during the SVB scareS004. USDC settled $21.5T of on-chain volume in Q1'26 (+263% YoY)S002 across 34 chainsS014. The mental model: Revenue ≈ (USDC circulation) × (reserve yield).

3. The reserve-income engine

96% of revenue is "reserve income" — interest on the assets backing USDC — which makes the income statement a direct function of the Fed funds rate.

Revenue comes from the interest on other people's money. Reserve income was 96% of FY25 revenueS021 (~94% in Q1'26S049); the largest non-reserve line, subscription & services, was just $34.9M in Q1'26S035. Reserves sit in the BlackRock-managed Circle Reserve Fund ($66.3B at YE25)S032, ~83% in Treasuries/repoS013. The reserve yield was 3.5% in Q1'26, down 66bps YoYS029.

4. The Coinbase economics — Circle's defining cost

The biggest cost-of-revenue line is a revenue-share to Coinbase, which takes roughly half of gross reserve income — a contractual ceiling on margin, not an inefficiency.

Under the 2023 Collaboration Agreement, Coinbase gets 100% of reserve income on USDC held on Coinbase, plus 50% of the residual off-platformS023. Circle paid Coinbase ~$1.4B in FY25S024 (from $924.5M restated FY24S065) — about 53% of gross reserve income ($1.4B ÷ $2.64B)S050. Total distribution/transaction costs were $1.66B in FY25S026, 58.6% of Q1'26 revenueS027. The offsetting lever: on-platform USDC of $13.7B (~18% of circulation)S034.

5. The market — a duopoly Circle does not lead

A ~$321B duopoly; Tether is ~2.5× USDC's size and vastly more profitable — that profitability gap is the central competitive fact.

The stablecoin market is ~$321B (near an all-time high)S005. Tether (USDT) is ~$189B, ~59% shareS006S007; USDC second at ~24%S008. Tether earned >$10B net profit in 2025S015 — vs Circle's GAAP loss — keeping all its float income and running a riskier reserve mix. Circle's edge is regulation: the first MiCA-compliant global issuer (EU, July 2024)S019.

6. Financial profile — GAAP loss, cash profit

FY25 was a GAAP loss driven by one-time IPO stock comp; the business generates cash, but quarterly earnings are already decelerating as the yield falls.

FY25 was a ~$70M GAAP loss on $2.75B revenue — driven by a ~$424M one-time IPO-vesting stock-based comp chargeS037. Underlying it is cash-generative: Q1'26 net income $55.2MS038, but down 15% YoY even as revenue grew 20%S063. Headcount ~1,100S036; zero corporate debt. Non-rate optionality is real but small: USYC $1.5BS039, CPN $8.3B annualizedS040, Arc presale $222MS064.

7. Regulation — the double-edged moat

The GENIUS Act gave Circle a federal seal of approval — and simultaneously invited every bank and fintech to compete, while banning the one thing (paying holders yield) that could widen the moat.

The GENIUS Act — the first U.S. federal stablecoin framework — was signed July 18, 2025 (PL 119-27)S046. It mandates ≥1:1 reserve backingS047, monthly disclosure and certification, and prohibits issuers from paying holders any yieldS048. Double-edged: it legitimizes compliant issuers (a moat vs offshore Tether) but invites bank/fintech entrants and caps "pay holders yield" as a growth lever. The competitive entry is already visible — the stock fell ~30% into late May 2026 on bank-stablecoin newsS071.

8. Investment lens & valuation

This sits in the tokenization thesis — real dollars moving onto blockchain rails — and Circle is the cleanest public, regulated way to own that migration. The question is not "are stablecoins growing" but "how much accrues to Circle's equity, net of the rate cycle and the Coinbase split." CRCL trades at ~10.0× EV/SalesS052 — richer than Coinbase (~9.1× P/S)S053, far above PayPal (~1.2× P/S)S055; only Robinhood is richer (~24.2×)S054.

9. Counter-thesis

The strongest opposing read is not "rates will fall" — it is that Circle is structurally a low-margin utility wearing a growth-stock valuation. Tether earns >$10B keeping all its float incomeS015; Circle nets a fraction because it pays ~53% of gross reserve income to CoinbaseS050 and is barred by the GENIUS Act from paying holders yieldS048. In this read, ~10× EV/SalesS052 on a single-product business whose revenue is ~94% a leveraged bet on the FedS049 — with Q1 net income already down 15% YoYS063 — prices the optionality (CPN, USYC, Arc) as if it were the business, when it is still rounding error. The bear doesn't need rates to crash; just the rate cycle to turn while Coinbase keeps its cut.
