Circle earns its revenue by investing the dollar reserves backing USDC — a $77B money-market-like float yielding ~3.5% — not by selling software; that model makes every dollar of earnings a leveraged bet on the Fed rate cycle, while Coinbase contractually captures ~53% of gross reserve income, creating a structural margin ceiling that the current ~10x EV/Sales multiple seems to price in neither the rate risk nor the Coinbase drag.

What it means

  • Circle is a regulated float business: it earns the yield on $77B of USDC reserves, not a software margin — revenue tracks the Fed funds rate almost 1:1, with a ±$756M swing per 100bps move.
  • Coinbase is Circle's defining cost: a 2023 collaboration agreement routes ~53% of gross reserve income to Coinbase, leaving Circle with a distribution-cost-to-revenue ratio of ~59% in Q1 2026 and no easy lever to close it.
  • USDC holds ~24% of the $321B stablecoin market; Tether's USDT is ~59% and earned >$10B in net profit in 2025 versus Circle's GAAP loss — the #1 player is both larger and structurally more profitable.
  • Q1 2026 net income fell 15% year-over-year even as revenue grew 20%; the GENIUS Act (July 2025) legitimizes Circle's compliance moat but also prohibits yield-to-holders and invites every bank and fintech to compete.
